If we are throwing the kitchen sink at Oracle v7.0 and letting gradient boosting sort out the signal from the noise, we cannot stop at technicals and fundamental earnings surprises.

The v6.0 dataset relied purely on structural price action, volume, and earnings dates. To build a true alpha engine for v7.0, we need to log and feed layers that capture macro regime shifts, sentiment velocity, and cross-asset institutional footprints.

### 1. Geopolitical News & Macro Sentiment Vector (The Missing Link)

You called this out directly, and it is the biggest blind spot for pure technical models. A perfect swing setup will get shredded instantly if a sudden tariff announcement, trade war escalation, or Middle East supply chain shock hits the wire.

* **News Sentiment Score (-1.0 to +1.0):** Real-time rolling sentiment of breaking news headlines specifically tagged to the ticker and its sector over the preceding 24–72 hours.
* **Macro Regime Index:** Daily composite score tracking yield curve slopes (2s10s spread), USD index velocity (DXY), and VEX/VIX volatility shifts. We want the Oracle to know *when* the broader macro tide is hostile to long swings.

### 2. Order Book Microstructure & Institutional Flow

Hourly bars tell us what happened, but they miss the internal plumbing of execution pressure.

* **Intraday Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) Distance:** How far is the entry price relative to the session or multi-session VWAP? Institutional accumulation happens quietly around VWAP; chasing too far above it increases trap probability.
* **Bid-Ask Spread & Order Book Imbalance:** Slippage eats swing trading alive. Tracking the rolling average spread and buy/sell order book depth at the moment of entry prevents entering illiquid traps.

### 3. Cross-Asset & Sector Relative Strength

Stocks rarely move in a vacuum; they get dragged by their sector boats or broad market tides.

* **Sector Relative Strength Ratio:** The rolling 14-period performance of the stock’s sector ETF (e.g., XLK, XLE, XLF) versus the S&P 500 ($SPY$). If the stock is trying to bounce while its sector is leaking institutional capital, the Oracle should flag it.
* **Beta to Index:** Dynamic trailing 30-day beta to SPY/QQQ so the model understands the volatility amplification of the candidate.

### 4. Comprehensive Path Dynamics & Excursion Metrics (The v6.0 Superpower)

Notice how your v6.0 training data includes columns like `MFE_Price` (Maximum Favorable Excursion), `Hold_Duration`, and multi-tier price/PnL paths (`Price_at_RSI_50`, `Price_at_RSI_45`, etc.).

* For v7.0, every single trade captured live in Parquet must track its **MFE** and **MAE** (Maximum Adverse Excursion) in real time while open.
* This teaches the Oracle not just *when* to enter, but how much heat a trade typically takes before reaching its RSI target.

### The Architecture for v7.0 Collection

To collect all of this without slowing down your hourly daemon, we can structure the Parquet feature store as a wide-format matrix. Every time `paper_trader.py` runs a scan, it appends a row containing:

1. **Identifiers:** Ticker, Sector, Watchlist Score.
2. **Technical State (v6.0 Core):** RSI, RVOL, ATR, Dist from EMA-200, Extreme RSI.
3. **Fundamental Context:** Days to/since earnings, last EPS surprise, beat/miss flag.
4. **New Macro & Sentiment Tier:** Geopolitical sentiment score, DXY momentum, sector relative strength.
5. **Execution & Path Labels (Populated upon trade closure):** Realized PnL, MFE, MAE, Hold Duration, Exit Reason.

By logging *both* taken trades and vetoed signals into this wide schema, Oracle v7.0 will train on an uncompromising,
high-dimensional dataset that bridges the gap between backtested theory and live market reality.

We pull it programmatically through lightweight, highly targeted API integrations so your hourly daemon never bogs down. Since you already use Python for your automation scripts, we can tap into robust, low-cost (or free) data providers to feed the Oracle v7.0 training store automatically.

The ingestion pipeline maps directly to these data sources:

* **Geopolitical & News Sentiment**: We can query free or low-cost NLP financial news APIs (like **Alpha Vantage News Sentiment**, **NewsAPI**, or lightweight RSS aggregators parsing Reuters/Bloomberg feeds) and run a quick Hugging Face sentiment pipeline or VADER scoring on headlines matching the ticker and sector for the last 24 hours.
* **Macro Regimes (Yields & DXY)**: Alpaca's historical and real-time data client doesn't just handle stocks—you can pull hourly bars for macro ETFs like **TLT** (20+ Year Treasury Bond), **UUP** (US Dollar Index tracking fund), and **VXX** (Volatility) to compute yield slopes and dollar velocity natively inside your existing loop.
* **Order Book & Microstructure**: Alpaca provides a quotes/trades endpoint (`StockLatestQuoteRequest`). While your hourly bars capture the price action, we can query the prevailing bid-ask spread at the exact minute of entry.
* **Sector Relative Strength**: We can map each stock ticker to its sector ETF (e.g., XLK, XLF, XLE) using your existing `sector_map` dictionary, pull its hourly bars alongside the stock, and compute a rolling 14-period relative strength ratio (`Stock_Close / ETF_Close`) on the fly.
* **Path Excursions (MFE/MAE)**: This data is generated completely internally. As a trade sits open in your portfolio, your daemon tracks its high and low watermarks every hour, automatically calculating Maximum Favorable Excursion and Maximum Adverse Excursion when the trade closes.

The comprehensive feature vector collected and streamed into `data/training_store/oracle_v7_live_training_store.parquet` with every hourly scan consists of the following 13 primary data points and metadata fields:

* **`Days_Since_Earnings`**: The precise day count elapsed since the most recent earnings announcement.
* **`Entry_Month`**: The numeric calendar month of the entry timestamp.
* **`Risk_Tier`**: The risk allocation tier applied to the trade setup (e.g., 0.02 for aggressive or 0.005 for tier setups).
* **`P_Trap` & `P_Whale**`: The real-time classification probability scores generated by the Oracle model.
* **`Action_Taken`**: The definitive engine decision recorded for the scan (**BOUGHT** or **VETOED**).
* **`Capture_Time`**: The exact Eastern Time timestamp of the evaluation or execution.
